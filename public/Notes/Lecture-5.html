Example 1(Torus of Revolution).

Take a circle S1(r) of radius r in the plane x=0 with a center (0,a,0). Rotate this circle around z axis, {x=y=0}.

Let p=(x,y,0). Then, d=a−x2+y2​. But, d2+z2=r2, so

(a−x2+y2​)2+z2=r2

Clearly, this is an equation that parametrises a torus. Thus, define a function

Clearly, a surface of a torus is defined by S=f−1(r2). To show that torus is a regular surface, we must show that r2 is a regular value for f. This is equivalent to demonstrating that dfp​=0∀p∈f−1(r2). Notice that

∂x∂f​=x2+y2​2x(x2+y2​−a)​,∂y∂f​=x2+y2​2y(x2+y2​−a)​,∂z∂f​=2z

Notice that if z=0 then automatically dfp​=0. Hence, let us consider the case when z=0. But the torus touches z axis only in the case when r=a, so assuming that a>r (which is usually the case for a torus), r2 is a regular value for f, hence S is a regular surface. When a=r, x2+y2​−a vanishes and hence torus will not be a regular surface.
