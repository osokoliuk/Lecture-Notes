Lemma DG7.4(Lemma).

If S is connected, N1​ and N2​ are unitary, normal vector fields on S then either N1​=N2​ or N1​=−N2​.

Proof.

∀p∈S, N1​(p), N2​(p) are unitary normal vector fields and N1​(p)=N2​(p) or N1​(p)=−N2​(p) (as there are only two normal directions). Let

S=A∪B,A={p∈S:N1​(p)=N2​(p)},B={p∈S:N1​(p)=−N2​(p)}

A and B are clearly disjoint and closed, since f±​(p)=N1​(p)±N2​(p) is a continuous function and a set of solutions f±​(p)=0 thus forms a closed set (since {0} is closed in R, as in the standard topology, R is Hausdorff and hence f±−1​(0) is closed whenever f±​ is). So, either A={∅} or B={∅} which is analogous to the statement that either N1​=N2​ or N1​=−N2​.□

Definition DG7.8(Definition).

Let

IIp​:Tp​S×Tp​S↦R(u,w)⇝−⟨dNp​(v),w⟩

We call IIp​ second fundamental form of S at p. Moreover, introduce

Ip​:Tp​S×Tp​S↦R(u,w)⇝⟨u,w⟩

It is a first fundamental form of S at p. Notice that since dNp​ is symmetric, it is diagonalisable. We can define k1​(p),k2​(p) to be the eigenvalues of −dNp​ with k1​(p)≤k2​(p). Eigenvalues are called principal curvatures of S at p. Additionally, we define K(p)=k1​(p)k2​(p)=det(dNp​) to be a Gaussian curvature and H(p)=(k1​(p)+k2​(p))/2 to be the mean curvature of S at p. The directions associated to the eigenvectors v1​ and v2​, which in turn have eigenvalues k1​ and k2​, are called principal directions. Finally, a curve α:I↦S is called a line of curvature if its tangent vector α′(t) is the principal direction of S at α(t)∀t∈I.
